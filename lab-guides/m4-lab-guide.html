Lab 1 · 30 minGraded · gate-map.md

Map Your Eval Gates

Before launch, Engineering ran Ascend IQ's evals on a test batch of answers and flagged six. Right now those evals only report a score, and nothing blocks a release. Decide which failures should become gates.

No gateThe eval got it wrong, and the answer is fine.
Advisory · L1Warns, and never blocks the release.
Soft · L2Pauses the release until someone signs off on a documented override. About 80% of gates live here.
Hard · L3Blocks the release until the issue is fixed. No override. For your most critical P0 risks.
Pull requestLightweight code-based checks that run on every change. Advisory or Hard.
Staging buildThe full suite, with LLM judges, on the golden set. Where most Soft gates live.
Release buildThe last line of defense. Hard gates only, as a final kill switch for P0 risks.
  1. Make your calls. Compare each answer with what's true and pick a gate. If it gets one, pick where it runs, and write one line on the business risk behind your call.
  2. Get pushback. Copy the prompt into any LLM. A skeptical engineering lead pushes back on your calls. Change the ones it convinces you on.
  3. Save. Note what changed, then copy the markdown over 04-eval-gates/gate-map.md, and commit.

Ascend IQ's clients are VPs who pay $50,000+ a year for verified data, and the CPO wants to unlock it for the top 50 accounts next month. The gate level follows that objective, not the metric: the same failure can be Soft in one product and Hard in another.

Lab 2 · 25 minGraded · launch-strategy.md

        
Write Your Release Criteria

        
Your gates are set. Now write them into the PRD as release criteria: the exact numbers the team checks before launch, and the plan if the Soft gate fails. You own the numbers. Engineering builds the pipeline that enforces them.

      


      

        
1 · MetricThe specific variable, such as Hallucination Rate. Never just “quality”.

        
2 · Gate severityHard, Soft or Advisory, so readers know at once whether it blocks launch.

        
3 · ThresholdThe exact pass/fail number, such as = 0% or < 2.0s.

        
4 · DatasetThe golden set it's tested on. This closes the “tested on easy data” loophole.

        
5 · MethodWhich evaluator runs the check. For your P0, the one in your Module 3 eval spec.

      


      
    
        
  1. Name the dataset. The golden set every criterion is tested on, and how many cases it holds.
